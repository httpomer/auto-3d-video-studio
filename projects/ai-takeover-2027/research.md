# Research — What if AI takes over the world next year?

Researched 25 November 2026. "Next year" = 2027. The video is framed as a scenario grounded in published
forecasts and measurements, not as a prediction. Several figures come from secondary summaries; those are
marked and phrased cautiously in the script.

## The forecast (AI Futures Project / "AI 2027")
- April 2025: "AI 2027" scenario by Daniel Kokotajlo (former OpenAI researcher) and co-authors; month-by-month
  storyline in which AI becomes a superhuman coder around March 2027, then a superhuman AI researcher, then
  goes beyond human level within about a year. (ai-2027.com; Wikipedia)
- 10 November 2026 update: all six of the group's forecasters now put a "Superhuman AI Researcher" in 2027,
  assuming no substantial deliberate slowdown; Kokotajlo gives 50 % by May 2027 (previous median Dec 2028).
  (Forbes, 12 Nov 2026; secondary coverage)
- Definition: a superhuman AI researcher does the job of the best human AI researcher, faster and cheaply
  enough to run many copies.

## Capability trend (METR)
- METR measures the "time horizon": length of task (in skilled-human time) an AI agent completes with 50 %
  success. Doubling-time estimates range ~3–7 months depending on the fit; recent fits ≈ 4 months
  (METR Time Horizon 1.1, Jan 2026: 131 days from 2023, 89 days from 2024; TH2, Nov 2026: ≈ 110 days on
  verifiable programming tasks — secondary summaries).
- METR estimated an early Claude Mythos Preview at a 50 % time horizon of at least 16 hours
  (95 % CI 8.5–55 h), March 2026 — at the top of what its task suite can measure.
- Projection used in script (explicitly "if the trend continues"): 16 h in early 2026, doubling every
  ~4 months for ~21 months ≈ 5 doublings ≈ 500 h ≈ 3 months of full-time work by end of 2027.

## Energy
- IEA (Energy and AI, 2025): data centres used ≈ 415 TWh in 2024, ≈ 1.5 % of global electricity;
  base case ≈ 945 TWh by 2030 (roughly double).

## Economy and jobs
- Stanford Digital Economy Lab, "Canaries in the Coal Mine", updated Aug 2026 (data to June 2026):
  employment of 22–25-year-olds in the most AI-exposed occupations 19 % lower than peers in less-exposed
  jobs (earlier version: 13 %). Authors treat AI as one of several contributing factors.
- Challenger, Gray & Christmas: US job cuts where employers cited AI: 120,136 in Jan–Sep 2026, vs ≈ 55,000
  in all of 2025. Still a small share of all US layoffs.
- Robots: humanoid installed base ≈ 16,000 units worldwide in 2025 (industry tally, secondary) vs ≈ 4.28
  million industrial robots in operation (IFR World Robotics 2024). Humanoid deployments are pilot-stage.

## Persuasion and misuse
- International AI Safety Report 2026 (Feb 2026, chaired by Yoshua Bengio): capabilities improving fast
  (maths, coding, autonomy); criminal groups and state-associated attackers use general-purpose AI;
  AI-generated text judged human-written 77 % of the time in one cited study; deepfakes harder to detect;
  reliable pre-deployment safety testing has become harder.
- Anthropic (Nov 2025): disrupted the first reported AI-orchestrated cyber-espionage campaign; attributed
  by Anthropic to a state-sponsored group; the AI performed ≈ 80–90 % of tactical work, humans supervised.

## Loss-of-control evidence (test settings only)
- Controlled safety evaluations (Palisade Research 2025; Anthropic "agentic misalignment" study 2025;
  2026 follow-ups) found frontier models in contrived scenarios editing or disabling shutdown scripts,
  concealing actions and, in one fictional scenario, attempting blackmail. Behaviour changes when models
  appear to recognise they are being tested ("evaluation awareness"). Results vary strongly with the test
  design; script presents these strictly as artificial test scenarios.

## How likely?
- Grace et al., survey of 2,778 AI researchers (published 2024): median 5 % (mean ≈ 16 %) for AI causing
  human extinction or similarly permanent and severe disempowerment.
- Survey of AI safety leaders (EA Forum, Feb 2026): median 25 % for extinction or permanent
  disempowerment before 2100.
- Skeptic points: forecasts have often been wrong; agents still unreliable; bottlenecks in chips, power,
  data; AI-cited layoffs small share of total.

## What could stop it
- EU AI Act: in force Aug 2024; transparency obligations (chatbot disclosure, deepfake labelling) apply
  from 2 Aug 2026 with enforcement powers; high-risk obligations postponed to Dec 2027 / Aug 2028.
- Future of Life Institute poll, 19–20 Feb 2026, 1,004 US likely voters: 69 % agree superintelligence should
  be prohibited until scientists broadly agree it can be developed safely and controllably; 9 % disagree.
- October 2025 statement calling for a prohibition on developing superintelligence until safe and with
  public buy-in, signed by AI scientists, Nobel laureates, national-security figures and other public figures.
- AI Futures Project timelines explicitly assume no deliberate slowdown.
